THE STORY SO FAR

Friday night the audit landed its core: a fidelity scoring engine, 10/10 new tests, the suite green at 321. Twenty minutes later the plan got interrupted mid-execution — I want you to rewrite — and at 1:14 a.m. Saturday the challenge went to the foundations: seems to me this flow is not good enough. The audit sided with you. All four audited sessions fit inside a single context window — worst case ~58k of 200k tokens — so the chunking, the overlap, and the machinery built to clean up after them were weight without work. The 3-event overlap drew the sharpest verdict on record: worse than noise — it’s negative value.

The replacement is two-phase deliberative reading: a draft read that holds provisional commits and open questions, then a retrospective revision once the whole session is known. An E1b control arm — sitting-sized chunks, zero overlap, richer carried header — is queued to isolate what actually helps, and E0, the first live run, is held until the topology proposal lands. While the reading was being rethought, the schedule proved itself: at 22:26 Friday the scheduler fired on its own for the first time, and by Saturday night it had digested nine catch-up sessions with nobody watching.

THE OPEN QUESTION

Deliberative reading is only proven on sessions that fit one context window. Does it hold when they don’t? E1b is the experiment that answers this.

What did the fidelity audit actually change?
brain · lens: feature / digest pipeline

Three things, in order of weight. Reading became two-phase — draft, then retrospective revision — because a session read once, linearly, kept committing to early wrong impressions. The 3-event overlap is gone, with the machinery that cleaned up its duplicates; the audit showed it produced negative value. And fidelity is now scored — the digest grades its own faithfulness to the raw transcript, so drift in the reader shows up as a number, not a feeling. Raw sessions are archived to .intent/ so every score can be re-checked against the source.
