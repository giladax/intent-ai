# Naming Exploration — replacing "Brain"

**Date:** 2026-07-05
**Status:** Exploration — 21 candidates, shortlist of 6, one recommendation.

## The brief

"Brain" was rejected as dull. The owner's naming philosophy, verbatim:

> "you want to ask yourself why did they call it like that, then think hard and then you
> convince yourself you understood but the name was mere nonsense that made you imagine."

Decoded: the name is an **open vessel**. It sounds like it means something, provokes
"why that name?", rewards theory-building with a satisfying-but-personal answer, and never
fully resolves. Evocative near-nonsense. The reference class is Slack, Discord, Figma,
Vercel, Notion-before-it-was-obvious — names people built a story around.

Hard exclusions: descriptive names (BrainHub, TeamMemory — dead on arrival), dull names,
names that explain themselves.

What the name must *resonate with* (without describing): an organization's engineering
memory that writes back. Sessions become evidence; every claim walks back to a raw
transcript line; an editorial feed of the org's work; you ask it and it answers with
receipts. Its soul: **alignment** (is what we built still what we decided?),
**provenance** (nothing asserted without evidence), **story** (it makes the org's work
readable).

## 1. Candidates (~21, across 6 strategies)

For each: the imagined "why did they call it that?" theory a user would build.

### Strategy A — Invented coinages (latinate / nordic mouth-feel)

**1. Sember**
*Theory a user builds:* "It's obviously remember with the front chopped off… or assemble
plus ember — the coals of the org's fire, still warm, still readable. They must have meant
that." (They didn't. It's a sound.)

**2. Kelda**
Old Norse for a spring, the source a stream rises from — but almost nobody knows that.
*Theory:* "Sounds like a Norse woman who keeps the well. The keeper of the source. The
source of truth is literally a spring you drink from." Users who dig find the real
etymology and feel like they cracked a code.

**3. Corven**
Pure coinage that smells of *corvid* — the remembering bird.
*Theory:* "It's a raven thing, right? Like Odin's ravens that fly over the world and
report back everything they saw. The product flies over our repos and reports back."
Never confirmed, never denied.

### Strategy B — Bookbinding & print (lifted, oblique)

**4. Quire**
A gathering of folded sheets before they're bound into a book — the org's pages, gathered
but never closed. Homophone of *choir* (many voices, one book) and buried inside
*inquire* / *acquire*.
*Theory:* "Ask Quire… a-quire… inquire! It's the thing you inquire of. Or is it the choir
— everyone's voice in one place?" Three competing theories, all satisfying, none official.

**5. Colophon**
The note at the end of a book stating how it was made — the original provenance record.
*Theory:* "Every book ends with the story of its own making. This is the colophon of our
codebase." Gorgeous, but three syllables and it fully resolves for anyone bookish.

**6. Deckle**
The rough, untrimmed edge of handmade paper — proof the page wasn't machine-cut.
*Theory:* "The raw edge is the evidence of how it was made. They kept the edges." Charming
but twee; sounds like a duck.

**7. Verso**
The left-hand page; the *other side* of the leaf.
*Theory:* "Flip the page over — the receipts are on the back." Collides with Verso Books;
resolves a bit fast.

### Strategy C — Navigation & surveying instruments

**8. Gnomon**
The blade of a sundial — the part that casts the shadow and makes time readable.
*Theory:* "It's the thing that turns raw time into something you can read. Wait — is the
G silent?" The pronunciation argument alone would give it a life of its own; also the
fatal flaw (gnome-on).

**9. Alidade**
The sighting arm of an astrolabe: point it at a thing, read the truth off the scale.
*Theory:* "You aim it at your org and it gives you a bearing." Beautiful and utterly
obscure, but three syllables and nobody can spell it.

**10. Kedge**
A small anchor you throw ahead, then haul the ship toward it.
*Theory:* "You move forward by pulling on what you already set down. Progress by hauling
on your own history." One syllable, verby, weird. Sounds slightly like a hedge fund.

**11. Plumb**
The plumb line — the instrument that tells you whether what you built is still true.
*Theory:* "Out of plumb = out of alignment. It checks the building against the plan."
Resolves *too* well — it's almost descriptive — and invites plumbing jokes forever.

### Strategy D — Geology & water (records laid down in layers)

**12. Varve**
One annual layer of lake sediment. Geologists count varves like tree rings — thousands of
years, readable layer by layer, each layer evidence of exactly what happened that year.
*Theory:* "Verve? Valve? Swerve? It sounds like it means something fast…" — and then the
one person who looks it up: "oh my god, it's a *sediment layer*. Every session deposits a
layer. You can core-sample the org." The perfect secret: invisible to most, devastating to
the few who dig.

**13. Tarn**
A small mountain lake left behind by a glacier — still water that remembers the ice.
*Theory:* "The glacier is gone but the tarn remains. The work moves on; the memory stays."
One syllable, nordic, calm. Maybe *too* calm for a product that writes back.

**14. Rill**
A tiny stream cutting its first channel. (The product's own internal language already
calls the event stream "the river.")
*Theory:* "Every big river starts as a rill. All these little session-streams feed one
river." Light, quick, one syllable — risk of reading as cute rather than deep.

**15. Moraine**
The ridge of debris a glacier leaves — you can read where it went by what it left behind.
*Theory:* "The trail of everything the work pushed ahead of itself." Evocative but sounds
like a woman's name, and "debris" is an unfortunate undertone for a memory product.

**16. Loam**
Rich soil made of everything that came before, decomposed into fertility.
*Theory:* "Old work breaks down into the soil the next thing grows in." Warm, one
syllable. Slightly inert — soil doesn't answer back.

### Strategy E — Music notation (marks that command return & alignment)

**17. Segno**
The sign in a score you jump back to — *dal segno*: "replay from the mark."
*Theory:* "It's the mark in the music you return to. Every claim has a mark; you can
always play it again from there." Walkable provenance as a musical instruction. Risk:
English speakers will say "SEG-no" instead of "SEN-yo" and argue about it (which is
half the fun).

**18. Ossia**
The alternative passage printed above the staff — "or, it could be played like this."
The gap between what was written and what was played.
*Theory:* "It's the intended line vs. the line we actually played — alignment as
notation." Deep cut; three syllables; probably too obscure even for the theory-builders.

**19. Clave**
The rhythmic key pattern in Afro-Cuban music that everything else must align to.
*Theory:* "It's the pattern the whole band locks onto. The org's clave." Alignment as
rhythm — lovely — but pronunciation splits (KLAH-veh vs. klayv) and "clave" is one letter
from a crypto wallet.

### Strategy F — Myth & records of the deed

**20. Skald**
The Norse court poet whose *job* was to remember deeds accurately — skaldic verse was
admissible as historical evidence precisely because the meter made lies hard to insert.
*Theory:* "The org hired a bard. It watches what we do and composes the true account."
One syllable, harsh in a good Skype/Slack way. Resolves for Norse-aware people — but into
a *story*, not a description, which keeps it alive.

**21. Anzu**
The Mesopotamian storm-bird who stole the Tablet of Destinies — the record of everything
decreed. Whoever holds the tablet holds what-was-decided.
*Theory:* Most users won't get anywhere near the myth — it just sounds great (vaguely
Japanese, two syllables, ends in a vowel like Figma/Miro). The 0.1% who find the Tablet
of Destinies will never shut up about it. Slight wrinkle: the bird *stole* the tablet.

*(Considered and cut before the list: Mimir — "ask Mimir" is mythically perfect (Odin
consults a preserved head that remembers everything) but God of War made the name famous
property of a beloved character; Coda — Coda.io owns it; Fathom — two well-known products;
Muninn — a known monitoring tool and too resolvable; Palimpsest / Mnemosyne / Saga /
Ledger — they explain themselves, which kills the magic.)*

## 3. Recommendation: **Varve**

### The case for

It passes the owner's test more exactly than anything else on the list. Said aloud it
sounds like a Vercel-class coinage — one hard syllable, no meaning offered, immediately
provoking "why did they call it that?" People will theorize: verve? valve? some founder's
dog? And then it hides a perfect secret: a varve is one year's layer of lake sediment,
laid down season by season, and geologists read thousands of years by counting them —
evidence, deposited in layers, walkable back to the exact stratum. That is this product's
soul (sessions deposit layers; every claim cores down to the raw line) expressed in a word
that *never volunteers it*. The 1% who look it up don't feel told — they feel like they
discovered it, and their theory is satisfying and personal, which is precisely the
philosophy verbatim. It also just works operationally: "check Varve," "Varve flagged
this," "ask Varve" all sound like sentences from a company that's existed for years.

### The honest case against

Three real risks. First, the ear: *Varve* sits one consonant from *Valve*, and Valve is
genuinely famous — in speech, "we use Varve" will occasionally get "the Steam people?"
Second, the resolution, once found, is *very* apt — a geologist colleague can stand up
and "solve" the name in one Slack message, and for that office the mystery collapses into
an explanation (though it collapses into a good story, not a feature list). Third, the
sound is cold — sediment, cores, clay; nothing in the mouth-feel says the product *writes
back and talks to you*. If the owner wants warmth or voice in the name, **Skald** (the
bard who answers) or **Quire** (the choir you inquire of) are the fallbacks; if the owner
wants maximum unresolvable emptiness, **Anzu**.

**Verdict: Varve** — nonsense at first hearing, a core sample underneath.

## DECIDED (owner, 2026-07-05): **Quire**

An unbound gathering of pages · a choir of voices · buried inside *inquire*. Three theories, no official one — per the philosophy, none will be confirmed. "Brain" is retired from all user-facing copy; internal code identifiers (brain_* MCP tools, brain tables) are renamed only when the API-break is worth it (flagged follow-up — the measurement campaign's treatment arm teaches brain_enter; rename after the campaign or coordinate both).
