# 2026-07-23 — What we want / What we have: the rebalance, the honesty, the day-zero flow

**Founder articulation, recorded near-verbatim with its implications. This is
the frame the next phase builds inside. It corrects a drift: we had become
too code-based.**

## The frame

- **What we want** is the north: docs, PRDs — things we *clearly and
  intentionally receive from the team*, including ideation done **with**
  them. These are the **deliberate truths**: the agreed-upon, final curated
  layer. Signing an epic. Signing a PRD. Authored by humans, analyzed by us,
  made authoritative by a signature.
- **What we have** is the output and reality of the org. **Code is one
  source of reality — the first, not the definition.** Future reality
  sources: spend, and whatever else the org's reality emits.
- **The tension between them is the product.** Everything Quire does is
  holding what-we-have against what-we-want and keeping humans aware of the
  gap.

The correction: reviews, diffs, repos are surfaces of *one* reality source.
The intent layer — authoring, ideation, curation, signing — is not an input
feature to the code product; the code analysis is one consumer of the
intent product.

## Why we always walk with the humans

### 1. No free lunch. We do not sell dreams.

Founder, verbatim spirit: *"We can't actually SOLVE this problem — let's
face it. No free lunch. Let's not sell dreams; I'm tired. We use LLMs to
help humans while the humans continue to make mistakes. The only important
thing is that the humans are accountable in the end. Mistakes will happen —
human awareness, proactive or reactive, is key."*

Implications, binding:

- **The product's promise is AWARENESS, not automated correctness.**
  Proactive awareness = the alarms, the taps on the shoulder, Needs-you.
  Reactive awareness = reviews, receipts, the ledger, the cross-examination.
  We guarantee the human *knew* — never that the org won't err.
- **Accountability is human and named.** Every authority act is a signature
  with a name on it. The system proposes with receipts; the human owns the
  call. This is why the SigningBlock is constitutional, not decorative.
- **Positioning honesty:** we never claim to prevent mistakes. We claim
  nobody discovers a broken promise six weeks late, and nobody can say they
  weren't told. That claim we can keep, every time.

### 2. The day-zero flow: born in Quire, not retrofitted

A team may start a brand-new feature with **no prior code**. They just want
to define what they want — ideation with us, drafted into the schema,
signed. Then, in the **nth-day flow, Quire is the source used to extend
the system**:

- the developer knows **what to develop**,
- QA and product know **what to test**,
- BI knows **what to analyze**.

Founder: *"We standardize how org knowledge is built. When this is the
case, we are golden — we are in a known and well-studied domain."*

Implications, binding:

- Onboarding has TWO doors, equal rank: **retrofit** (paste a repo, mine
  its promises — the O1 hero flow) and **greenfield** (define what you
  want first; code arrives later and is born governed). The greenfield door
  is the stronger form of the Jira kill: the ticket isn't imported — the
  intent is *born here*, and everything downstream inherits it.
- The R&D roles (dev, QA, product, BI) are known consumers with known
  needs — the fixed-schema thesis again: we serve a well-studied domain,
  we don't invent an ontology.
- Sessions-as-intent (O4) and the authoring surface (PM writes a PRD, we
  analyze — ruled 2026-07-23) are pieces of this door, not add-ons.

## What this changes in emphasis (flags, not edits)

1. **Intent surfaces gain rank.** Authoring/ideation/signing flows are
   product core, not onboarding chrome. The Feature definition page is as
   much "what we want" (the signed intent, its history) as "what we have"
   (reviews, sessions).
2. **The review is one awareness surface, not the center.** The center is
   the want/have tension; code review is where that tension is visible for
   the code reality source.
3. **Reality sources are a plural, open set.** Architecture should treat
   "code" as source #1 of N (spend and others later) — naming and contracts
   shouldn't hard-wire code-ness where reality-ness is meant.
4. **The demo should show both doors** if it can: retrofit (paste URL) AND
   a taste of greenfield (define a promise first, watch work inherit it).
5. **PRD next revision absorbs this frame** alongside the schema-moat and
   PM-kill theses (see 2026-07-23 strategy memo).
